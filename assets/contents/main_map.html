The letter with no return address

The Hunt for the Hidden Word

Somewhere between the Santa Susana Mountains and the Simi Hills, a valley is keeping a secret it has kept for a very long time.

The story begins with a letter. It arrives with no return address — only a hand-drawn map of nine places in Simi Valley and one line of instruction: Nine objects. Nine letters. One word. Go in any order you like.

The letter is signed by a woman who called herself the Valley Archivist. For forty years she collected the things this valley almost forgot: a coin from a famous speech, a strip of film from a western that was never released, a lantern from a ranch kitchen, a whistle from the last freight train, a basket woven the old way, a key cut from sandstone, a bell from the hilltop cross, a mask from an empty stage, and a seed medallion from the park where the valley comes to rest on Sunday afternoons.

Before she disappeared, she hid all nine. Not to lose them — to make someone look.

Here is what she understood. You cannot simply carry these objects out of the valley. Each one is guarded by a piece of language. To take an object, you have to earn it: you have to think in English quickly and confidently, the way you will need to think when you stand in front of a room and pitch your idea.

So the hunt is also a training ground. At every location you learn one piece of a system built for adults who need results, not grammar lectures. Then you play for the object until the language stops feeling foreign.

There is no correct route, and the Archivist was clear about that. Start at the library on the hill, or the ranch in the rocks, or the old depot by the tracks — it makes no difference. Some hunters begin with the puzzle that looks easiest. Some begin with the place they can already picture. The valley keeps no score on the order.

Every object you win carries a letter, and the letters mean nothing on their own. Collect all nine and they arrange themselves, and the word they spell is the name of the place that has been under your feet the entire time.

Nine locations. Nine objects. Any order. Open the map and choose your first.
