Why you already know more English than you can speak

You have read hundreds of English words this month. You have understood emails, dashboards, product docs, LinkedIn posts. And still, in a meeting, the sentence arrives late — or never. That gap is not a knowledge problem. It is a retrieval problem.

This method fixes retrieval by attaching every new word to a movement or an image before you try to store it as a rule. It is called TPR — Total Physical Response — and it is the reason a child learns "jump" in one afternoon and an adult learns it in three weeks.

The three-step loop

See the word → attach a movement or picture → recall it fast, out loud. Repeat until the recall is faster than the translation. A word that survives that loop behaves like a native word: it shows up when you need it.

What this changes in a business meeting

  • You stop translating. Translation costs two seconds, and two seconds is the difference between leading a discussion and following it.
  • You practise production, not recognition. Recognising "forecast" on a slide is free. Saying "our forecast slipped" is the skill worth training.
  • You use fewer rules. Nine ideas cover almost everything in this system. The rest is repetition with movement.
