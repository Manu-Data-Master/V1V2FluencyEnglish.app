Two Kinds of Verb

Hidden here: Ironwood Film Reel 🎞️ — clear ten rounds and it is yours.

Every English verb is one of two characters

Corriganville was a film ranch. Westerns were shot in these rocks, and every western needs two characters: the boss who never explains himself, and the old man who cannot cross the street without a cane. English verbs cast the same two roles.

V1 The boss

am · is · are · was · were · will be

Strong. Asks its own questions — no help required. Only allows a noun, a feeling or a situation next to it.

Our pricing is competitive. → Is our pricing competitive?

V2 The one with the cane

launch · sell · report · hire · ship…

Weak. Cannot ask a question alone. It borrows a helper: Do, Does, Did, Will.

We launched the campaign. → Did we launch the campaign?

The hand test — two seconds, always works

Can you do it with your hands or your body? Then it is V2 (send, present, negotiate, build). Can't you? Then you are looking at V1 — the verb "to be" and nothing else.

The same idea, both ways
StatementTypeQuestion
The demo is ready.V1Is the demo ready?
The team ships on Friday.V2Does the team ship on Friday?
They were in the meeting.V1Were they in the meeting?
Marketing approved the budget.V2Did marketing approve the budget?

Why this one idea carries the rest

Once you can name the character, you already know how to ask, how to answer and where to put "not". Locations 4 and 5 give you the exact forms. This location trains the split until it is instant.
