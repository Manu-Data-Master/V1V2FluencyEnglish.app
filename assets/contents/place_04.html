Six words, three times, no exceptions

A depot runs on a timetable, and V1 is the timetable of English. It has six forms in total. Learn the grid below and the entire "to be" family is finished — present, past and future.

V1 forms and how to flip them into questions
TimeStatement — S + V1 + NQuestion — V1 + S + N?
PresentI am / you-we-they are / he-she-it isAm I…? Are you…? Is he…?
PastI-he-she-it was / you-we-they wereWas I…? Were you…?
Futurewill be — every subjectWill you be…?

The question is a swap, not a new sentence

Take the statement and move V1 to the front. The report is ready → Is the report ready? Nothing else moves. This is why V1 needs no helper: it is strong enough to lead.

Yes, no, and where "not" lives

  • Yes: S + V1 + N. — Yes, they were in the call.
  • No: S + V1 + not + N. — No, they weren't in the call.

"Not" always sits immediately after V1. Never before it, never at the end.

When an action verb stands next to V1

A V2 cannot act next to the boss, so it changes costume and becomes a situation: add -ing or -ed. We are presenting on Thursday. The proposal was approved. In both cases the word after V1 behaves like a noun, exactly as the frame requires.

Meeting phrases built on V1 alone

I am ready. The numbers are final. That was our best quarter. The client is not convinced. We will be online at nine.
