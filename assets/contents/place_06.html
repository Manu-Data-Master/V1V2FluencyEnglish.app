Five words that decide how you sound in a room

The trail up Rocky Peak has five gates, and each one asks a different question: may I pass, should I pass, must I pass. English has the same five, and choosing the wrong one is how a polite request lands as a demand.

Same verb, five different intentions
HelperWhat it doesIn a meeting
CanEveryday permission or abilityCan I share my screen?
CouldThe same request, softerCould you send me the deck?
MayFormal permissionMay I add one point before we close?
ShouldAsking for or giving an opinionShould we raise the price?
MustObligation, no negotiationWe must deliver before the launch.

Helper + S + V2 + N ?

They behave exactly like Did / Do / Will

They go to the front, and the verb after them stays plain. Can she join? — not "can she joins". Negatives keep the same position: We should not discount this deal.

The register trap

Should asks for an opinion. Must states a requirement. Say "you must send the invoice today" to a client and you have given an order; say "you should send the invoice today" and you have given advice. In English the difference is the whole sentence.

Fast rule of thumb

Asking a favour upwards? Could. Asking a colleague? Can. Speaking to a room of executives? May. Recommending? Should. Stating a deadline? Must.
