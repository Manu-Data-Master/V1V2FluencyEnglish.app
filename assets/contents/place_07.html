Five small rules that clean up your English

From the cross on this hill you can see the whole valley at once. Same idea here: five short rules, each fixing a mistake that professionals repeat for years.

1 · Place and time: under, on, at, in

Which one, and why
WordUse it forExample
AtAn exact point or hourThe call starts at 2:00.
OnA surface or a dayThe figures are on the slide. We ship on Monday.
InInside something, or a month or yearThe data is in the report. We launch in March.
UnderBelow somethingThe total is under budget.

2 · Adjectives describe nouns, adverbs describe actions

An adjective sits next to a thing. An adverb — usually ending in -ly — sits next to a V2. The angry client (adjective) answered angrily (adverb). In business writing: a quick decision vs we decided quickly.

3 · "Let's" + V2 — the meeting verb

Let's is short for "let us", and it is the fastest way to move a room. Formula: Let's + V2 (+ noun) — Let's start. Let's review the numbers. Let's take this offline.

4 · Since vs from … to

  • Since = one starting point: since Monday, since 2019, since the merger.
  • From … to … = two ends of a period: from Monday to Friday, from January to June.

5 · Words ending in -tion

Most of them are near-twins of the word you already use in your first language: information, presentation, negotiation, production. Two things to remember: they are nouns, so they pair with V1 — the presentation is ready — and the ending sounds like "shun", never "tee-on".

Why these five and not fifty

Each one shows up in almost every professional conversation you will have. Fix these and your English stops sounding translated.
